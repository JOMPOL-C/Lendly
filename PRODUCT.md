# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Lendly serves customers who want to rent costumes online, shops that manage costume listings and rental workflows, and admins who monitor customers, rentals, returns, chat, and tracking. The primary product context is task-driven: users search, compare, register, rent, pay, message, review, and manage orders rather than browse a pure marketing site.

## Product Purpose

Lendly is an online costume rental platform that brings discovery, sizing, rental availability, checkout, return workflows, reviews, and shop/customer communication into one web experience. Success means customers can confidently find and rent the right costume, while shops can manage inventory and rental issues with less manual coordination.

## Positioning

A trustworthy costume rental workspace where browsing, booking, communication, payment, and return handling stay connected from first search to completed rental.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, stylish, and dependable. The interface should feel approachable enough for first-time renters, polished enough for fashion/costume discovery, and structured enough for rental operations that involve dates, payment, identity, chat, and returns.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic marketplace clutter, over-decorated ecommerce visuals that bury the task, inconsistent button/form styles across screens, low-contrast pastel text, and admin pages that feel disconnected from the customer-facing experience. Visual effects should support confidence and orientation, not become decoration.

## Design Principles

- Keep rental confidence visible: availability, sizing, price, status, payment, and return information should be easy to find before users commit.
- Make browsing feel lightweight but not vague: product cards and category pages should encourage exploration while preserving clear actions.
- Preserve one component vocabulary across customer, shop, and admin flows so the platform feels like one product.
- Use warmth and softness selectively; operational screens need clarity, readable contrast, and stable layouts first.
- Design every form state deliberately, especially registration, login, payment, upload, validation, and rental issue flows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target accessible web UI patterns with keyboard-friendly navigation, visible focus states, readable Thai and English text, sufficient color contrast, and reduced-motion alternatives for animated surfaces. Avoid relying on color alone for rental status, errors, payment outcomes, or admin alerts.
